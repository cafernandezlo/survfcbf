\textbf{Motivation:} Filter feature selection for high-dimensional survival
data is dominated by rankers, which at their conventional cut-off keep thousands of
genes, many of them near-duplicates. The fast correlation-based filter (FCBF) prunes
each feature that an approximate Markov blanket renders redundant and returns a
compact panel, but no FCBF implementation accepts a censored outcome, and the
reference benchmark of survival filters includes none.\\
\textbf{Results:} \pkg{survfcbf} carries FCBF to right-censored data through
martingale residuals of a null Cox model, with its core validated against the
original FCBF package (identical selections on 395 datasets). It ships the filter in
two cardinality modes, a leakage-safe nested cross-validation harness, a survival
simulator with cluster-level ground truth and wrappers for standard baselines. In its
recommended mode the user sets the panel size, ten genes in our runs. On eleven TCGA
cohorts and METABRIC it runs near-linearly in the number of features (8\,s at
$p = 20{,}000$ on synthetic data) and completes every fold. Over ten repetitions of
cross-validation its ten-gene panels discriminate less well than the rankers'
two-thousand-gene lists, by about 0.05 in truncated Uno's C, with the same gap in
METABRIC. At equal panel size it is practically equivalent to mRMR in discrimination
and halves the within-panel redundancy of unpruned mutual information, at a cost in
stability.\\
